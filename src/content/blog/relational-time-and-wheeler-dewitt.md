In classical Newtonian mechanics, time is treated as an immutable, external parameter—a universal clock ticking steadily in the background regardless of what matter or energy does. General relativity shattered that absolute frame by treating spacetime as a dynamic, malleable manifold. Yet in standard quantum mechanics, time remains stubbornly external: the Schrödinger equation evolves states according to a fixed $t$:

$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\Psi\rangle = \hat{H} |\Psi\rangle$$

When you attempt to combine general relativity and quantum mechanics canonically via the Dirac quantization of the ADM (Arnowitt-Deser-Misner) action, something extraordinary happens: the Hamiltonian constraint vanishes.

### The Vanishing Hamiltonian

Because general relativity is a diffeomorphism-invariant gauge theory, the total Hamiltonian is a linear combination of constraints. Applying Dirac's operator quantization yields the famous **Wheeler-DeWitt equation**:

$$\hat{\mathcal{H}} |\Psi\rangle = 0$$

There is no time derivative $\partial / \partial t$ anywhere on the right-hand side. The quantum state of the entire universe is static. This apparent paradox is known as the **Problem of Time**.

### Mach, Barbour, and Relational Dynamics

Is the universe truly frozen? No. The resolution lies in realizing that time was always an epiphenomenon. Clocks do not measure "time"; clocks are physical systems that change relative to other physical systems.

In the relational formulation pioneered by Ernst Mach and formalized mathematically by Julian Barbour and Bruno Bertotti, dynamics is formulated purely in configuration space $\text{Riem}(\Sigma) / \text{Diff}(\Sigma)$ (superspace). 

Instead of asking *"Where is particle $A$ at time $t$?"*, we ask *"What is the configuration of particle $A$ when particle $B$ is at position $X$?"*. The relational distance metric between neighboring field configurations is given by:

$$ds_{\text{rel}} = \sqrt{2(E - V)} \sqrt{\sum_i m_i d\vec{x}_i \cdot d\vec{x}_i}$$

Duration $\delta t$ is derived after the fact from the sum of all internal displacements:

$$\delta t_{\text{rel}} = \frac{ds_{\text{rel}}}{2(E - V)}$$

When we strip away the illusion of an external clock, the universe does not begin or end—it evolves through relational geometry.
